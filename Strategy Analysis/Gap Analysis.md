# Gap Analysis (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

Gap Analysis is a technique used by Business Analysts to **compare the current state (As-Is) with the desired future state (To-Be)** in order to identify gaps that must be addressed to achieve business objectives.

A gap represents the **difference between where the organization is today and where it wants to be**.

Gap Analysis answers the question:  
**“What needs to change for us to reach the desired state?”**

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Organizations often define ambitious future goals without fully understanding:
- Current limitations
- Missing capabilities
- Required changes

Without gap analysis:
- Initiatives are under-scoped
- Effort and cost are underestimated
- Critical changes are overlooked

Gap Analysis exists to help a BA:
- Make change requirements explicit
- Identify scope and effort accurately
- Support roadmap and planning decisions
- Reduce implementation risk

BABOK emphasizes Gap Analysis because **effective strategy requires understanding the distance to the target state**.

## 4. When to Use Gap Analysis

Gap Analysis is most effective when:
- Defining transformation initiatives
- Planning system replacements or upgrades
- Supporting business cases
- Designing future-state processes or systems
- Responding to regulatory or market changes

Common BA scenarios:
- Digital transformation programs
- Legacy system modernization
- Process improvement initiatives
- Regulatory compliance changes

---

## 5. Types of Gaps

### Capability Gaps
Missing skills, tools, or systems

### Process Gaps
Inefficient, manual, or missing process steps

### Technology Gaps
Legacy systems or lack of automation

### Data Gaps
Missing, inconsistent, or low-quality data

### Compliance Gaps
Failure to meet regulatory or policy requirements

---

## 6. How to Apply Gap Analysis – Step by Step

### Step 1: Define the Current State (As-Is)
Document:
- Existing processes
- Systems and tools
- Capabilities and constraints

### Step 2: Define the Future State (To-Be)
Describe:
- Desired capabilities
- Target processes
- Expected outcomes

### Step 3: Identify Gaps
Compare As-Is vs To-Be to identify:
- Missing capabilities
- Required changes
- New requirements

### Step 4: Assess Impact and Effort
Evaluate:
- Complexity
- Cost
- Risk
- Dependencies

### Step 5: Prioritize Gaps
Rank gaps based on:
- Business value
- Urgency
- Feasibility

### Step 6: Define Actions
Translate gaps into:
- Requirements
- Initiatives
- Roadmap items

## 9. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Defining vague or high-level gaps
- Skipping current state analysis
- Underestimating effort and dependencies
- Treating gaps as solutions
- Failing to validate findings with stakeholders

---

## 10. Real-World Example (Banking / IT)

Scenario: Manual customer onboarding

Current state:
- Paper-based forms
- Manual verification
- Long turnaround time

Future state:
- Digital onboarding
- Automated verification
- Faster customer activation

Gap Analysis identifies:
- Technology gaps
- Process redesign needs
- Data integration requirements

These gaps become scoped requirements and initiatives.
