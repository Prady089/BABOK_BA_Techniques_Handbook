# Prototyping (BABOK® Technique)

## What This Technique Is
Prototyping creates a **representation of a solution** (often UI/UX) to clarify requirements, validate understanding, and gather feedback early.

Prototypes can be:
- Low-fidelity: sketches, wireframes
- High-fidelity: clickable mockups
- Proof-of-concept: technical prototype (usually owned by dev/arch)

## Why This Technique Exists
Users often struggle to explain needs in words.  
Seeing a prototype:
- reduces ambiguity
- exposes missing requirements
- accelerates consensus

## When to Use
- User-facing applications
- Complex workflows
- High risk of misunderstanding
- When stakeholder alignment is difficult

## How to Apply (Step-by-Step)
1. Select scope (screen, workflow, feature)
2. Create a low-fidelity draft first
3. Review with users and capture feedback
4. Iterate to refine key flows
5. Confirm decisions and document requirements

## Benefits
- Faster feedback loop
- Improved usability
- Reduced rework

## Limitations
- Stakeholders may assume prototype = final solution
- Can bias design too early if used improperly
- Needs time-boxing and governance

## Related Techniques

## Key Takeaway
Prototyping is a communication tool that reduces requirement ambiguity dramatically.
