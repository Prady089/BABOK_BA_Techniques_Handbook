# Change Control (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

Change Control is a technique used by Business Analysts to **manage, evaluate, approve, and track changes to requirements** throughout their life cycle.  
It ensures that requirement changes are **controlled, assessed for impact, and aligned** with business objectives before implementation.

Change Control answers the question:  
**“What happens when a requirement needs to change?”**

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Change is inevitable in any initiative. Without structured control, changes can lead to:
- Scope creep
- Budget and timeline overruns
- Misalignment between stakeholders
- Rework and quality issues

Change Control exists to help a BA:
- Maintain integrity of baselined requirements
- Support informed decision-making
- Balance flexibility with governance
- Reduce delivery and compliance risks

BABOK emphasizes Change Control because **unmanaged change is a common cause of project failure**.

## 4. When to Use Change Control

Change Control is most effective when:
- Requirements are baselined
- Multiple stakeholders are involved
- Regulatory or contractual obligations exist
- Solutions are complex or long-running
- Delivery is phased or iterative

Common BA scenarios:
- Banking and financial systems
- Regulatory change initiatives
- Vendor-managed implementations
- Enterprise platforms

---

## 5. What Constitutes a Change

A change may include:
- Addition of a new requirement
- Modification of an existing requirement
- Removal of a requirement
- Change in priority, scope, or acceptance criteria

Not all changes have the same impact — some are **minor**, others are **significant**.

---

## 6. Change Control Process – Step by Step

### Step 1: Capture the Change Request
Document:
- Description of the proposed change
- Reason and business justification
- Requestor details

### Step 2: Perform Impact Analysis
Assess impact on:
- Scope
- Cost
- Schedule
- Risks
- Dependencies
- Solution design

### Step 3: Evaluate the Change
Determine:
- Business value vs effort
- Regulatory or contractual necessity
- Alignment with objectives

### Step 4: Approve or Reject the Change
Decision-makers may include:
- Product owner
- Sponsor
- Change Control Board (CCB)

### Step 5: Update Artifacts
If approved:
- Update requirements
- Update traceability
- Update plans, backlogs, and designs

### Step 6: Communicate the Decision
Ensure all stakeholders understand:
- What changed
- Why it changed
- When it takes effect

## 9. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Allowing informal or undocumented changes
- Skipping impact analysis
- Treating all changes as equal
- Failing to update traceability
- Poor communication of decisions

## 10. Real-World Example (Banking / IT)

Scenario: Mid-project regulatory update

A new reporting field is mandated after requirements are baselined.

Change Control ensures:
- Impact is assessed
- Trade-offs are reviewed and approved
- Requirements and designs are updated consistently
- Compliance risk is managed
