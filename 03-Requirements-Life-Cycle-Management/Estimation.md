# Estimation (BABOK® Technique)

## What This Technique Is
Estimation is the practice of predicting **effort, time, cost, complexity, or resources** needed to deliver a requirement or solution.

BAs use estimation to support:
- planning and forecasting
- trade-off discussions
- prioritization and roadmap decisions

## Why This Technique Exists
Stakeholders need commitments, but uncertainty is real.  
Estimation provides **informed forecasts** (not guarantees) that improve decision-making.

## When to Use
- Project planning and roadmap creation
- Release planning and budgeting
- Prioritization and scope trade-offs
- Vendor / build-vs-buy comparison

## Common Estimation Approaches
- **Expert judgment**: estimates from SMEs/engineers
- **Analogous estimation**: compare to similar past work
- **Parametric estimation**: based on measurable units
- **Three-point estimation (PERT)**:
  - Optimistic (O), Most likely (M), Pessimistic (P)
  - Expected = (O + 4M + P) / 6
- **Relative estimation** (Agile):
  - story points, t-shirt sizing

## How to Apply (Step-by-Step)
1. Define estimation scope and unit (time/cost/points)
2. Clarify assumptions and constraints
3. Break work into smaller items (decomposition)
4. Collect estimates from relevant roles
5. Validate against historical data (if available)
6. Document confidence level and risks
7. Re-estimate as understanding improves

## Outputs
- Estimates with assumptions
- Range estimates (best/likely/worst)
- Inputs to business case and planning

## Common Mistakes
- Treating estimates as fixed commitments
- Estimating without clarified scope
- Ignoring risk buffers
- Using a single number instead of a range

## Related Techniques
- Functional Decomposition
- Risk Analysis
- Decision Analysis
- Prioritization

## Key Takeaway
Good estimation communicates uncertainty clearly and supports realistic planning.
