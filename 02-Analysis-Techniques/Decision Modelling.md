# 10.17 Decision Modelling

## 1. Purpose
Decision Modelling is used to represent how a repeatable business decision is made. It provides a clear and unambiguous way to show the logic, data, and knowledge used to reach a specific outcome.

## 2. Description
This technique separates business rules and logic from processes, making them easier to understand, manage, and automate. A popular standard for decision modeling is the **Decision Model and Notation (DMN)**, which provides a graphical language for modeling decisions.

## 3. Key Elements (DMN)
- **Decision:** The act of determining an output from a number of inputs, using decision logic. Represented by a rectangle.
- **Input Data:** The information used as an input to one or more decisions. Represented by an oval.
- **Business Knowledge Model:** A representation of business knowledge (like a set of rules) that can be invoked by decisions. Represented by a rectangle with clipped corners.
- **Information Requirement:** Shows that a decision requires certain input data or the output of another decision. Represented by a solid arrow.
- **Knowledge Requirement:** Shows that a decision invokes a business knowledge model. Represented by a dashed arrow.

## 4. Decision Logic Representation
The logic inside a decision is often represented using **Decision Tables**, which provide a tabular way to express a complex set of business rules.

## 5. Usage
- To clarify complex business logic.
- To enable the automation of business decisions.
- To manage business rules independently from processes and applications.
- To ensure consistency and transparency in decision-making.
