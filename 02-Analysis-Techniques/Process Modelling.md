# Process Modelling (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

Process Modelling is a technique used by Business Analysts to **visually represent how work is performed** within an organization.  
It shows the **sequence of activities, decision points, roles, inputs, and outputs** that make up a business process.

Process models help stakeholders understand **how work actually flows**, not just how it is supposed to flow.

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Organizations rely on processes, but many processes are:
- Poorly documented
- Understood differently by different teams
- Inefficient or error-prone
- Dependent on manual workarounds

Process Modelling exists to help a BA:
- Create a shared understanding of current workflows
- Identify inefficiencies, bottlenecks, and risks
- Support process improvement and automation
- Serve as a foundation for requirements and design

BABOK emphasizes process modelling because **clear processes enable clear requirements**.

---

## 3. When to Use Process Modelling

Process Modelling is most effective when:
- Improving or redesigning business processes
- Implementing new systems
- Replacing or enhancing legacy systems
- Addressing operational inefficiencies
- Supporting compliance and audit initiatives

Common BA scenarios:
- Banking operations workflows
- Claims or payments processing
- Customer onboarding
- Regulatory reporting processes

---

## 4. When NOT to Use Process Modelling

Process Modelling may add limited value when:
- The work is simple and informal
- Steps are highly discretionary
- The process is already optimized and stable

In such cases, **lightweight documentation** may be sufficient.

---

## 5. Common Types of Process Models

### High-Level Process Models
Provide an overview of major process stages without detail.

### Detailed Process Models
Show step-by-step activities, decisions, and handoffs.

### As-Is Process Models
Represent how the process currently operates.

### To-Be Process Models
Represent the desired future-state process.

## 6. Common Process Modelling Notations

### Flowcharts
Simple and widely understood visual representations.

### BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation)
Standardized notation for complex and cross-functional processes.

### Swimlane Diagrams
Processes organized by roles, systems, or departments.

---

## 7. How to Apply Process Modelling – Step by Step

### Step 1: Define the Scope
Clarify:
- Start and end points
- Process boundaries
- Level of detail required

### Step 2: Identify Stakeholders and Roles
Determine:
- Who performs each activity
- Which systems are involved

### Step 3: Capture the As-Is Process
Elicit:
- Activities
- Decision points
- Inputs and outputs
- Exceptions and workarounds

### Step 4: Create the Process Model
Visually represent:
- Sequence of steps
- Decisions and branching paths
- Roles or swimlanes
- Hand-offs between teams or systems

### Step 5: Validate with Stakeholders
Review the model to:
- Confirm accuracy
- Resolve discrepancies
- Ensure shared understanding

### Step 6: Identify Improvements
Analyze the process to find:
- Bottlenecks
- Redundancies
- Risks and control gaps
- Automation opportunities

### Step 7: Define the To-Be Process
Model the improved future state incorporating agreed changes.

## 9. Outputs

Typical outputs include:
- As-Is and To-Be process models
- Identified inefficiencies and risks
- Inputs to requirements and solution design
- Improved stakeholder alignment

---

## 10. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Modelling at the wrong level of detail
- Ignoring exceptions and edge cases
- Mixing system design with business process logic
- Failing to validate models with stakeholders
- Treating process models as static artifacts

---

## 11. Real-World Example (Banking / IT)

**Scenario:** Payment processing delays

Process modelling reveals:
- Multiple manual approval steps
- Duplicate data entry
- Unclear ownership between Operations and IT

Using the process model:
- Bottlenecks are removed
- Controls are optimized
- Automation opportunities are identified
