# 10.34 Process Analysis

## 1. Purpose
Process Analysis is used to understand and assess a business process for its efficiency and effectiveness. The goal is to identify opportunities for improvement.

## 2. Description
This technique involves a systematic review of a process to understand its components, including its inputs, outputs, activities, and the resources it consumes. By analyzing the "as-is" process, business analysts can identify bottlenecks, redundancies, and gaps, which informs the design of an improved "to-be" process.

## 3. Key Activities
- **Process Identification:** Defining the boundaries of the process to be analyzed (start and end points).
- **Information Gathering:** Collecting data about the current process through observation, interviews, and document analysis.
- **Process Mapping/Modelling:** Creating a visual representation of the as-is process (e.g., using flowcharts or BPMN).
- **Analysis:** Evaluating the process against various metrics, such as:
    - **Time:** How long does it take?
    - **Cost:** How much does it cost to execute?
    - **Quality:** How many errors or defects does it produce?
    - **Value:** Does each step add value for the customer?
- **Identifying Improvements:** Pinpointing specific areas for change, such as simplifying steps, automating tasks, or eliminating waste.

## 4. Usage
- To support business process improvement (BPI) initiatives.
- To identify the root cause of operational problems.
- To define requirements for technology solutions that will support a new process.
- To ensure a process is aligned with business goals and customer expectations.
