# 10.20 Financial Analysis

## 1. Purpose
Financial Analysis is used to assess the financial viability, stability, and profitability of a business, project, or investment. It helps stakeholders understand the financial aspects and implications of a proposed change or solution.

## 2. Description
This technique involves evaluating costs, benefits, and the overall value of a solution. It can range from simple cost-benefit comparisons to complex financial models. The goal is to provide a clear, data-driven basis for making investment decisions.

## 3. Key Metrics & Concepts
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA):** Comparing the total expected costs against the total expected benefits to see if the benefits outweigh the costs.
- **Return on Investment (ROI):** A performance measure used to evaluate the efficiency of an investment. `ROI = (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) * 100`.
- **Net Present Value (NPV):** The difference between the present value of cash inflows and the present value of cash outflows over a period of time. A positive NPV indicates a profitable investment.
- **Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** The discount rate that makes the NPV of all cash flows from a particular project equal to zero.
- **Payback Period:** The time it takes for an investment to generate enough cash flow to recover its initial cost.

## 4. Usage
- Justifying a project in a business case.
- Comparing different solutions or investment options.
- Assessing the financial performance of an existing solution.
- Supporting buy-vs-build decisions.
