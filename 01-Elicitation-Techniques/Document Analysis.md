# Document Analysis (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

Document Analysis is a technique where the Business Analyst **reviews existing documentation** to understand current processes, rules, data, decisions, and constraints.  
These documents represent the **official or intended way** an organization operates.

Document Analysis is often one of the **first techniques used** when a BA joins a new initiative or domain.

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Organizations already capture large amounts of knowledge in documents, such as:
- Policies
- Procedures
- Reports
- Specifications
- Regulations

Document Analysis exists to help a BA:
- Quickly build domain understanding
- Identify stated requirements and constraints
- Understand regulatory and compliance expectations
- Prepare effectively for interviews and workshops

BABOK emphasizes this technique because **documents reflect organizational intent**, even if reality differs.

---

## 3. When to Use Document Analysis

Document Analysis is most effective when:
- Working in regulated industries
- Replacing or enhancing legacy systems
- Joining an initiative midstream
- Preparing for elicitation sessions
- Understanding formal processes and rules

Common BA scenarios:
- Banking and financial services
- Healthcare and insurance
- Government and compliance-driven projects

---

## 4. When NOT to Use Document Analysis

Document Analysis may not be sufficient when:
- Documents are outdated or inaccurate
- Work is largely undocumented
- Stakeholders rely on tribal knowledge
- Actual processes differ significantly from documented ones

In such cases, **observation and interviews** must be used to supplement findings.

---

## 6. How to Apply Document Analysis – Step by Step

### Step 1: Identify Relevant Documents
Select documents based on:
- Business scope
- Regulatory requirements
- System or process coverage

### Step 2: Assess Document Quality
Determine:
- Currency and version
- Ownership
- Intended audience
- Reliability

### Step 3: Review with Purpose
Focus on:
- Business rules
- Inputs and outputs
- Decision points
- Exceptions and constraints

### Step 4: Extract Key Information
Capture:
- Requirements
- Assumptions
- Definitions
- Gaps or inconsistencies

### Step 5: Identify Questions and Gaps
Note:
- Conflicting statements
- Missing steps
- Ambiguous terminology

### Step 6: Validate with Stakeholders
Confirm findings through:
- Interviews
- Workshops
- Observation

---

## 9. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Assuming documents reflect reality
- Ignoring document versioning
- Treating all documents as equally reliable
- Failing to validate interpretations
- Over-relying on documents without stakeholder input

---

## 10. Real-World Example (Banking / IT)

**Scenario:** Anti-Money Laundering (AML) system upgrade

- Policies define monitoring thresholds
- Procedures outline escalation steps
- Regulatory guidance specifies reporting timelines

Document Analysis reveals:
- Inconsistent thresholds across documents
- Missing exception handling steps

These findings drive targeted interviews and workshops.
