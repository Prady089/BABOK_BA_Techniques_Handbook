# Concept Modelling (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

Concept Modelling is a technique used by Business Analysts to **identify, define, and relate key business concepts** within a domain.  
It focuses on **what things mean to the business**, not how systems store or process them.

A concept model visually represents:
- Business concepts
- Relationships between concepts
- Shared understanding of terminology

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Misunderstandings in projects often arise because:
- Different stakeholders use the same term differently
- Concepts are assumed but never defined
- Business and IT interpret entities inconsistently

Concept Modelling exists to help a BA:
- Create a shared business vocabulary
- Remove ambiguity from discussions
- Align stakeholders before detailed analysis
- Provide a foundation for data and requirements models

BABOK emphasizes concept modelling because **clarity of meaning precedes clarity of requirements**.

---

## 3. When to Use Concept Modelling

Concept Modelling is most effective when:
- Working in complex domains
- Multiple systems share common data
- Terminology is inconsistent or overloaded
- Starting large or enterprise-level initiatives
- Supporting data-heavy solutions

Common BA scenarios:
- Banking and financial services
- Insurance and healthcare
- Enterprise platforms
- Regulatory initiatives

---

## 4. When NOT to Use Concept Modelling

Concept Modelling may not add value when:
- The domain is simple and well understood
- Terminology is already standardized and stable
- Detailed data models already exist and are agreed

In such cases, **data modelling alone** may be sufficient.

---

## 5. Key Elements of a Concept Model

- Business concepts (nouns)
- Clear definitions
- Relationships between concepts
- Business-focused language
- No technical or implementation details

Concept models avoid:
- Data types
- Attributes
- Database structures

---

## 6. How to Apply Concept Modelling – Step by Step

### Step 1: Identify Core Concepts
Extract nouns from:
- Business discussions
- Documents
- Regulations
- Requirements

### Step 2: Define Each Concept
Write:
- Clear, concise business definitions
- Language understandable to non-technical stakeholders

### Step 3: Identify Relationships
Determine how concepts relate:
- Ownership
- Association
- Dependency

### Step 4: Create the Concept Diagram
Visually represent:
- Concepts as nodes
- Relationships as connecting lines

### Step 5: Validate with Stakeholders
Review the model to:
- Confirm definitions
- Resolve terminology conflicts
- Ensure shared understanding

---

## 7. Inputs

Typical inputs include:
- Business objectives
- Domain documentation
- Stakeholder interviews
- Existing glossaries or data models

---

## 8. Outputs

Typical outputs include:
- Concept model diagrams
- Agreed business definitions
- Inputs to data models and requirements
- Improved stakeholder alignment

---

## 9. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Mixing technical data details into concept models
- Using system-specific terminology
- Overcomplicating the model
- Skipping stakeholder validation
- Treating concept models as final data designs

---

## 10. Real-World Example (Banking / IT)

**Scenario:** Trade processing platform modernization

Different teams use terms like:
- Trade
- Transaction
- Position

Concept modelling clarifies:
- What a trade represents
- How it relates to positions and accounts
- Where responsibilities lie

This prevents downstream data and integration issues.
