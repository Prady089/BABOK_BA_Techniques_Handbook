# Focus Groups (BABOK® Technique)

## What This Technique Is

A Focus Group is a facilitated discussion involving a **selected group of stakeholders** who share similar roles, characteristics, or experiences.  
It is used to gather **qualitative insights, perceptions, opinions, and expectations** about a product, process, or problem area.

Unlike workshops, focus groups are **exploratory**, not decision-driven.

---

## Why This Technique Exists

Some insights emerge only through **group interaction**, where participants:
- Build on each other’s experiences
- Reveal shared pain points
- Surface unspoken expectations

BABOK includes Focus Groups to help Business Analysts:
- Understand stakeholder perceptions
- Identify common needs and concerns
- Discover themes that may not emerge in one-on-one interviews

---

## When to Use Focus Groups

Use focus groups when:
- Understanding user experience is critical
- Requirements are still forming
- Feedback from a specific user segment is required
- Exploration is more important than decisions

Common scenarios:
- Customer-facing systems
- Policy or process changes
- Product or service enhancements

---

## How Focus Groups Are Conducted

1. Identify a representative participant group  
2. Define discussion objectives and guiding questions  
3. Facilitate open discussion without forcing consensus  
4. Capture themes, patterns, and insights  
5. Analyze findings and validate key observations  

---

## Advantages

- Encourages shared insights
- Reveals group dynamics
- Efficient way to gather multiple perspectives

---

## Limitations

- Dominant voices may influence discussion
- Outputs are qualitative, not definitive
- Not suitable for detailed requirement validation

---

## Example (Banking / IT)

Conducting a focus group with branch staff to understand customer onboarding challenges before redesigning the onboarding process.

---

## BABOK Knowledge Areas Supported

- Elicitation & Collaboration
- Strategy Analysis

---

## Key Takeaway

Focus groups are best used for **exploration and understanding**, not final decisions.  
They complement interviews and workshops by revealing collective perspectives.
