# Business Case (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

A Business Case is a technique used by Business Analysts to **justify an investment or initiative** by evaluating its expected benefits, costs, risks, and alternatives.  
It provides decision-makers with a **structured rationale** for approving, rejecting, or prioritizing an initiative.

A business case answers the question:  
**“Why should we do this?”**

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Organizations have limited resources and competing priorities.  
Without a business case:
- Decisions rely on intuition or influence
- Benefits are unclear or overstated
- Risks are underestimated
- Investments lack accountability

The Business Case exists to help a BA:
- Align initiatives with strategic objectives
- Compare alternatives objectively
- Make assumptions explicit
- Enable informed, defensible decisions

BABOK emphasizes business cases because **value realization must be intentional**.

## 4. When to Use a Business Case

A Business Case is most effective when:
- Proposing a new initiative or investment
- Requesting funding or approval
- Comparing solution options
- Supporting transformation or modernization
- Prioritizing initiatives at portfolio level

Common BA scenarios:
- Core system replacement
- Regulatory compliance programs
- Automation initiatives
- New product or service launches

---

## 5. Key Components of a Business Case

A typical business case includes:
- Problem or opportunity statement
- Business objectives
- Options considered (including “do nothing”)
- Cost estimates
- Expected benefits (quantitative and qualitative)
- Risks and assumptions
- Recommendation

---

## 6. How to Create a Business Case – Step by Step

### Step 1: Define the Problem or Opportunity
Clearly state:
- What issue exists
- Why it matters
- Who is impacted

### Step 2: Identify Objectives
Define what success looks like and how it aligns with strategy.

### Step 3: Identify Options
Include:
- Preferred option
- Alternative options
- Do-nothing option

### Step 4: Estimate Costs
Consider:
- One-time costs
- Ongoing operational costs
- Implementation and change costs

### Step 5: Identify Benefits
Capture:
- Financial benefits (cost savings, revenue)
- Non-financial benefits (risk reduction, compliance, customer experience)

### Step 6: Assess Risks
Identify:
- Delivery risks
- Operational risks
- Regulatory or compliance risks

### Step 7: Compare Options
Evaluate options using:
- Cost-benefit analysis
- Risk assessment
- Strategic alignment

### Step 8: Make a Recommendation
Clearly state:
- Recommended option
- Rationale
- Key assumptions

---

## 9. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Overstating benefits
- Ignoring risks or assumptions
- Excluding the do-nothing option
- Failing to align with strategy
- Treating the business case as static

---

## 10. Real-World Example (Banking / IT)

Scenario: Automating manual reconciliation

Options:
- Maintain manual process
- Partially automate
- Fully automate

Business case evaluates:
- Cost savings from reduced manual effort
- Risk reduction from fewer errors
- Implementation cost and timeline

Decision-makers approve the option with highest net value and acceptable risk.
