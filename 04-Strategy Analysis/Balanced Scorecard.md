# 10.3 Balanced Scorecard

## 1. Purpose
The Balanced Scorecard technique is used to manage performance in any business model, organizational structure, or business process. It provides a balanced view of an enterprise by measuring performance across four key perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Business Process, and Learning & Growth.

## 2. Description
This framework helps organizations translate their strategic objectives into a set of coherent, linked performance measures. Instead of relying solely on financial metrics, it includes leading indicators of future performance, ensuring that the drivers of success are managed.

## 3. Key Elements
- **Financial Perspective:** How do we look to shareholders? (e.g., profitability, revenue growth, ROI).
- **Customer Perspective:** How do customers see us? (e.g., satisfaction, market share, brand loyalty).
- **Internal Business Process Perspective:** What must we excel at? (e.g., operational efficiency, quality, cycle time).
- **Learning and Growth Perspective:** How can we continue to improve and create value? (e.g., employee skills, technology, innovation culture).

## 4. Usage
- Aligning business activities to the strategy.
- Communicating the strategy to the entire organization.
- Tracking and managing the execution of strategic objectives.

## 5. Example
A software company might track:
- **Financial:** Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR).
- **Customer:** Net Promoter Score (NPS).
- **Internal Process:** Average bug resolution time.
- **Learning & Growth:** Number of employees certified in a new technology.
