# Risk Analysis (BABOK® Technique)

## 1. What This Technique Is

Risk Analysis is a technique used by Business Analysts to **identify, assess, and manage potential risks** that could impact the success of an initiative.  
A risk is an uncertain event that, if it occurs, can have **positive or negative impact** on objectives.

Risk Analysis answers the question:  
**“What could go wrong (or right), and how do we handle it?”**

---

## 2. Why This Technique Exists

Initiatives fail not only due to poor requirements, but because:
- Risks are identified too late
- Impact is underestimated
- Mitigation actions are unclear

Risk Analysis exists to help a BA:
- Anticipate threats and opportunities early
- Reduce surprises during delivery
- Support informed decision-making
- Improve confidence in plans and commitments

BABOK emphasizes Risk Analysis because **proactive risk management protects value**.

## 4. When to Use Risk Analysis

Risk Analysis is most effective when:
- Planning new initiatives or transformations
- Making investment decisions
- Working in regulated or high-impact environments
- Integrating multiple systems or vendors
- Managing tight timelines or budgets

Common BA scenarios:
- Core system replacement
- Regulatory programs
- Vendor selection
- Large-scale integrations

---

## 5. Types of Risks

### Strategic Risks
Risks affecting alignment with business objectives.

### Operational Risks
Risks related to processes, people, or day-to-day operations.

### Technical Risks
System, integration, performance, or security risks.

### Compliance Risks
Regulatory, legal, or policy-related risks.

### Data Risks
Data quality, availability, or integrity risks.

---

## 6. Risk Analysis Process – Step by Step

### Step 1: Identify Risks
Use:
- Workshops
- Interviews
- Historical data
- Lessons learned

### Step 2: Categorize Risks
Group risks by type:
- Strategic
- Operational
- Technical
- Compliance
- Data

### Step 3: Assess Likelihood and Impact
Evaluate:
- Probability of occurrence
- Severity of impact

### Step 4: Prioritize Risks
Rank risks using:
- Risk matrix
- Scoring models

### Step 5: Define Mitigation Actions
Decide to:
- Avoid
- Mitigate
- Transfer
- Accept

### Step 6: Monitor and Review
Track risks throughout the initiative and update as conditions change.

## 9. Common Mistakes by Business Analysts

- Treating risk analysis as a one-time activity
- Ignoring positive risks (opportunities)
- Underestimating impact
- Failing to assign ownership
- Not tracking mitigation actions
